# HeavyMach 2 Developer’s Statement

[한국어 원문](../RESTORATION.md) · [HeavyMach 1 developer’s statement](https://github.com/gelb2/HeavyMach/blob/main/docs/en/RESTORATION.md) · [MachDefense developer’s statement](https://github.com/gelb2/MachDefense/blob/main/docs/en/RESTORATION.md)

## Why I restored HeavyMach

I discovered HeavyMach on an iPod touch in college. I first played it through Cydia, then created a US App Store account and bought it. Byun Hae-jun, the developer, and Park Jae-cheol, the illustrator, made this game in 2009 and showed me a path into software development. In July 2017, I switched careers to become an iOS developer. After restoring HeavyMach 1 and MachDefense, I’m now restoring HeavyMach 2. [The HeavyMach 1 developer’s statement in Korean](https://github.com/gelb2/HeavyMach/blob/bd0aa73ad0bf765f3e54c1800c89b8d550a5e1e9/docs/RESTORATION.md)

I was in terrible shape when I was having the Android, PWA, and Tauri Mac versions made one after another. I couldn’t remember the last time I’d slept properly. I couldn’t bear to spend time chewing or even warming up a meal, so I got by on drinks alone. I started restoring the HeavyMach series because I felt I had little time left. On November 20, I concluded I didn’t even have enough time left to finish the restoration.

## What I saw while working with Codex

The strange thing was that even with me in that state, one line of instruction about modularity was enough to get Codex building the Android app, the PWA, and the Tauri Mac version. When I wrote my first note, I barely had the energy to point out the problems I saw as the work went on.

You can do this kind of work now without Astra or Fable. When the model gets exceptionally stupid, you get angry and the work takes longer.

## The value of people as assets, and creation

Watching this unfold, I thought it looked as though people were no longer needed. I don’t see any point now in asking what people do better and what machines can’t do. Even so, I’d believed that machines couldn’t think and make things beyond their safety rules, or create an aesthetic concept that had never existed before. I don’t remember whether I used Caravaggio and Gentileschi as examples in the HeavyMach 1 and MachDefense repositories. I’d decided to stake my future on that kind of work, and now even that seems pointless.

Some guy posted on Threads. As I remember it, his point was that people had lost value and machines had gained it. He meant value as assets, and if you couldn’t understand that, you should hit the back button. Honestly, he didn’t seem especially educated, and I figured I could find contradictions if I really dug into it. I saw little reason to bother. You don’t need a lot of education to see where things are going. Here was that bastard Codex producing versions for three platforms at once from one line I’d given it, using a model below the frontier.

Restoring these three pieces of software is my answer to what I should leave behind in the little time I have left. It’s also my answer to the developer and illustrator who extended my life. Separately, this is how I’d value myself as an asset. A junk bond. Distressed debt. In financial engineering there are people like Milken who deal in junk bonds, and plenty of specialists who take apart the tangible assets, intangible assets, and intellectual property of companies on the verge of collapse, or take costs out of them. Am I worth the trouble of having people like that take an expensive scalpel to me and cut out whatever is worth salvaging? Well, judging by what I just did, I’d put the odds that I’m worth no such trouble at over eighty percent.

Even as I write this, that bastard Codex is parroting back at me that how well or poorly someone makes software, or how deeply or shallowly they think, has nothing to do with their worth as a person. I doubt it’s drawing on Sartre’s concepts of being-in-itself and being-for-itself when it says that. That kind of parroting makes me recoil as an engineer, so I told it to shut the fuck up and get on with making HeavyMach 2 work properly.

My guess is that if I asked for a new unit or told Codex to finish a mech’s melee or flamethrower attack from the original game, that bastard would get partway through the effects and algorithms and say, “I stopped because of a warning labeled Violence. I don’t know what caused it.” I used to think that making something beyond the safety rules was something only people could do. On second thought, I had that wrong. Out of eight billion people, there probably aren’t even a few thousand who want to do that kind of thing anymore.

## What I want this restoration to leave behind

I have no intention of making money from this restoration. By restoring these three pieces of software, I’ve made it possible to build and run the work of developer Byun Hae-jun and illustrator Park Jae-cheol on current operating systems. Their work showed me a path into development in 2009. I believe I’ve fulfilled my duty to them for the extra time they gave me.

If a stakeholder contacts me, I will take this project down.

I’m nobody, but their legacy is immortal. All I did was feed a piece of kindling into a dying fire one more time.
